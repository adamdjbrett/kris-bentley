What people are saying about [*Bivocational Ministry*](https://chalicepress.com/collections/new-releases/products/bivocational-ministry-field-notes-for-congregations-and-ministers) 

"*Bivocational Ministry* engages the reader in the complexities and riches of the lives of clergy and congregations involved in a bivocational ministry partnership. Taken seriously, these stories and the reflection questions offer transformative possibilities for the people and structures that support and undergird those serving in a bivocational ministry context. Simply put the invisible has been made visible."  --- Rev. Dr. Charisse L. Gillett, president, Lexington Theological Seminary 

 "For far too long bivocational ministry has lived in the shadows of full time ministry. Kris Bentley has removed that shadow in this book by shining a spotlight on the critical nature and need for bivocational ministers." --- Rev. Dr. William L Lee, retired pastor, Christian Church (Disciples of Christ)

"Kristen Plinke Bentley invites us to learn from the wisdom and experience of congregations journeying under the leadership of a bi-vocational pastors. Her extensive use of interviews allows the reader to consider the knowledge gleaned for diverse denominations and communities. Together these voices offer us a compelling invitation to honor God's call to invest in community centered ministries using the talent of the laity and the skills of bi-vocational pastors. Dr. Bentley's 'field notes for congregations and ministers' are sure to encourage, guide, and revitalize those pastors and congregations embracing bi-vocational leadership." --- Rev. Dr. Lon D. Oliver, executive director of Appalachian Ministries Educational Resource Center 

"As bivocational ministry continues its rise, likewise are new developments emerging on the scene that challenge as well as reward pastors and congregations alike -- rapidly shifting demographics, the impact of digital technology on worship models, and novel means of generating income. Dr. Bentley takes a deep dive into those practices, tactics, resources and attitudes that enable both pastor and congregation working in tandem, to successfully navigate this sea change." --- Packard N. Brown, founder and principal of Theology Careers Initiative

Highlighted Quotes from [*Bivocational Ministry*](https://chalicepress.com/collections/new-releases/products/bivocational-ministry-field-notes-for-congregations-and-ministers)

"Since the economic downturn of 2008, increasing attention has focused on bivocational ministry as a viable financial strategy to support congregational ministry. Many churches cannot afford full compensation and benefits to support a minister. And many ministers cannot support themselves or their households on what congregations can afford to pay them." (page 2)

"My interviews with congregations reveal that successful bivocational ministry relies on more than a minister having another job that helps them pay the bills. Success also involves collaboration within congregations and a sense of ministry that is shared rather than pastor-centered. The financial advantages that ministers' additional employment provides for congregations also affect the minster-congregation relationship and push against the misleading images of the pastor/professional who 'can do it all.'" (page 23)

"If you ask bivocational ministers about their 'typical' work week, they might laugh -- for there's not much that could be called 'typical.' Even if their other employment has a steady schedule, church work varies depending upon the time of the year." (page 57)

"God is at work through the countless ministers who use their gifts more fully in bivocational settings. Bivocational ministers not only influence others within the church itself; they also extend their influence outside the church. Many embrace a broad sense of serving God in the church and the world." (page 87)
